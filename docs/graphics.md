# Graphics: indexed sprites and backgrounds

Every picture in the cartridge, the backgrounds as well as the sprites, is
stored as **palette indices**, never as RGB. The pictures are generated by
Python scripts from geometric primitives. No bitmap is drawn by hand, and
none is taken from another game.

```
tools/geometry.py      court size, camera, projection (shared with az_core.h through G_*)
tools/palette.py       the fixed colours, the firmware's quantiser, the cell solver
tools/art_court.py     the stadium and court picture of each surface, the net sprite
tools/art_cities.py    the fourteen cities: names, rivals, surface, skies, skylines
tools/art_sprites.py   players (9 poses × 2 views), ball, shadows, logo, trophy, confetti, board font
tools/art_common.py    colour helpers, reduction to a palette, run-length coding, firmware font
tools/build_art.py     runs all of the above → src/gen/art.inc, assets/png/*.png
```

`python3 tools/build_art.py` is deterministic: it needs Pillow and the PRG32
checkout (for its 8 × 8 font). The same inputs give a byte-identical
`art.inc`, which is committed, so building the cartridge does not need Python
art tools.

## The colour model

The two kinds of PRG32 host keep the screen differently:

* The **ESP32-C6** keeps an **8-bit indexed framebuffer**: each pixel is an
  index into a 256-entry system palette. The firmware maps any RGB565 colour
  that a cartridge draws to a cell of a 6 × 6 × 6 colour cube
  (`prg32_gfx_index_for_rgb565_unlocked`).
* **QEMU** keeps a direct-colour RGB565 framebuffer.

A cartridge may set any cell of the system palette to an exact colour with
`prg32_palette_set(cell, rgb565)`. Exact colours therefore come for free, on
one condition: **two colours on screen at the same time must not fall into
the same cube cell**. `tools/palette.py` meets that condition:

* `cube_index()` is an exact port of the firmware's quantiser.
* `solve()` gives every colour its own cell:
  * a colour whose cell is free keeps its value;
  * a colour almost equal to the colour already in its cell takes that colour;
  * any other colour moves to the nearest colour of the nearest free cell.
* `check()` proves that no two colours of a palette share a cell.
  `build_art.py` asserts it for every city, and `python3 tools/palette.py`
  checks the fixed colours.

At run time, `city_load()` in `azzurra.c`:

1. computes each compact colour's cell with the same quantiser;
2. sets the cell to the exact colour (`prg32_palette_set`);
3. draws with `prg32_gfx_rect_indexed(…, cell)`.

Busy rows are drawn as 8-bit sprites whose palette holds the same RGB565
values, so they quantise to the same cells.

`tests/test_game.c` keeps both framebuffers on the host and requires that the
indexed one, shown through the system palette, equals the direct one pixel
for pixel, on every screen and in every city.

## The compact palette

Each screen uses one compact palette of 98 entries (`PALETTE_SIZE`). For each
city, `city_palette[city * 98 …]` holds the final RGB565 values.

| Indices | Count | Use |
|--------:|------:|-----|
| 0–31 | 32 | Sprite colours. Index 31 ("clear") is transparency and is never drawn. |
| 32–47 | 16 | Stands, seats, sponsor boards, wind screen, net, posts, chair, HUD. |
| 48–57 | 10 | The city's surface colours: court inside and outside (two tones each), line and line shade, wear, edge, and the rival pair's shirt and its shade. |
| 58–97 | up to 40 | The city's skyline. |

How each kind of picture refers to these colours:

* **Sprites** keep 5-bit indices: the first 32 colours.
* **Court pictures** keep 6-bit indices: colours 0 to 57. The courts are
  shared by every city on the same surface, and the city's own surface
  colours repaint them.
* **Skylines** keep 6-bit *local* indices, which `view_map[city * 64 + local]`
  translates to compact indices.

**Tints.** A character's colours are swapped by a 32-entry tint table, with
no extra sprite data:

* the hair (Azzurra blonde, Viola brown, the rivals varied);
* the skin tone;
* the rivals' shirt (the city's `S_RIVAL`/`S_RIVALD`).

## Run-length rows

Every picture is coded one row at a time (`encode_rows` in `art_common.py`):

* **Each byte** is `(index << (8 − bits)) | run`.
* **A run of 0** means that the next byte holds a longer run.
* **Rows are shared:** identical rows are stored once, and many rows of the
  stands and the court are identical.

Row offsets are `uint16_t` arrays: `view_rows`, `court_rows`, `spr_rows`.

| Data | Rows | Bits/index | Bytes |
|------|-----:|-----------:|------:|
| 14 skylines, 320 × 40 | 560 | 6 (local) | 24,361 |
| 3 courts, 320 × 150 | 450 | 6 | 15,490 |
| 35 sprites | 1,079 | 5 | 7,600 |

The skylines are reduced to their palette without dithering: dithered
versions took twice the space (46–49 KB) and did not look better at this
size.

## Sprites

| Sprite | Size | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| Player, back view (near team) | 34 × 58 | 9 frames: ready, run 1, run 2, backhand, hit, follow-through, toss, smash, cheer |
| Player, front view (far team) | 22 × 36 | The same 9 frames, drawn from the front at 0.62 scale and mirrored for the backhand |
| Ball | 6 × 6 | Scaled by depth to at least 3 px; with a shadow ellipse on the court |
| Net | full width | A see-through checker mesh with a white tape, drawn as a sprite so that depth sorting puts it between the far and near players |
| Sponsor boards | 3 slots | RISC-V, UNIPARTHENOPE, PRG32, OPEN-HW, OPEN-SW in a 3 × 5 board font; they rotate between points |
| Logo | — | "AZZURRA'S / PRO TENNIS / CHALLENGE", gradient, outline and shadow |
| Trophy | 44 × 62 | With confetti in six colours |

How the sprites are drawn:

* **Scaling.** Players and the ball are scaled by depth, using the integer
  projection `az_px`.
* **Depth sorting.** The sort key is the player's ground y. The net sits
  between the far and near players. A smashed or lobbed ball, high in the
  air, is drawn in front of everything near it.

## Text

The HUD and every message use the PRG32 firmware's own 8 × 8 font.
`firmware_font()` reads it from
`PRG32/components/prg32/prg32_display_qemu_rgb.c` and stores it as 64 glyphs
from ASCII 32. Text is drawn on a 40 × 25 character layer:

* **Panels:** a dark panel behind each run of text.
* **Big text:** `text_big`, doubled in size, for titles.
* **The HUD row:** offset to y=1 so that it fits the 10-pixel bar.

## Review sheets

`build_art.py` also writes two contact sheets:

* `assets/png/cities.png`: every skyline and court.
* `assets/png/sprites.png`: every sprite.

`tests/run_tests.sh shots` renders every screen of the real cartridge code on
the host to `build/shots/*.png`. `scripts/qemu_preview.py` grabs the same
screens from the QEMU firmware.
